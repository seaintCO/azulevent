The questions long-term private investors ask of operating businesses

A practical look at business quality, customer concentration, succession and stewardship for owners seeking patient capital.

By Ocean Azul Partners Editorial · October 22, 2026 · 5 min read

Long-term private capital can evaluate a company differently from investors seeking a quick exit. The exact priorities vary, but patient investors often spend significant time understanding the durability of a business and the quality of the people responsible for running it.

Durability begins with the customer. Are revenues recurring or repeatable? Do a few accounts represent most sales? What would happen if the largest customer left? A company with a modest growth rate and diversified demand may be attractive for different reasons than a high-growth business built on one contract.

Operating discipline matters alongside demand. Investors commonly review financial controls, capital spending, incentives, management depth and key-person risk. Businesses that depend entirely on the founder can face difficult transitions even if the founder has built an excellent product.

The ownership structure is another essential part of diligence. Governance rights, debt covenants, partner agreements and historical share issuances affect what a prospective investor would actually acquire and how future decisions would be made.

Succession planning is not only an issue for retirement. Clear responsibilities and documented processes make a company more capable of expanding, absorbing acquisitions or withstanding an unexpected leadership change.

A thoughtful investment conversation should therefore cover how the company earns, keeps and reinvests capital. Valuation matters, but the ability to steward that business through multiple market conditions matters as well.
